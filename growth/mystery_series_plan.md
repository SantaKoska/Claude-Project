# New series (proposal, 27 Nov 2026): mystery stories in parts

Owner's ask: real mysterious incidents (Kerala, India, world) told as stories, with fear, in several parts with cliffhangers, so people wait for the next part and subscribe.

## The psychology we use (and its limits)
- **Curiosity gap (George Loewenstein's information-gap idea):** curiosity is the itch between what we know and what we want to know. Open one sharp question in the first 2 s; never close it in the same part.
- **Open loops / Zeigarnik effect:** unfinished things stay in the mind. End each part *in the middle of the moment* (cut on the scream, the knock, the torch going out), not after the resolution. The effect is debated in research and depends on context, so we test it with our own retention numbers.
- **Suspense, not shock:** the viewer sees the danger before the character does (the shadow between the trees while she keeps walking). Dread lasts; jump scares don't. No gore: what we don't show is scarier, and blood cuts reach on both platforms.
- **"This really happened":** truth is the strongest fear. So every fact is sourced; legends are labelled as legends.
- **Appointment viewing:** same time every night (9 PM suits fear). End card states the schedule as information: "PART 2 · TOMORROW 9 PM". No "follow / like / share" asks (house rule).
- **3-second recap:** each part opens with "previously" so a new viewer is not lost, then goes back to Part 1 (profile visits -> follows).
- **Platform tools:** Instagram Reels linking (Part 2 links back to Part 1), YouTube Shorts related-video link to the next part, a pinned playlist, identical covers with "PART 1/3".
- Evidence: platform coverage + practitioner reports; the big growth numbers online are anecdotes. We judge by our own data (Part 2 views / Part 1 views, follows per reel).

## Format
- 3 parts per story, 60-75 s each, posted on 3 nights in a row at 9 PM.
  - Part 1: place, year, the person, the night; rising dread; the encounter -> cut to black.
  - Part 2: the morning, the discovery, the village, the first theories -> ends on one strange clue.
  - Part 3: what investigators / scientists found, the twist, what is still unexplained; callback to Part 1's first line.
- Narration: slow, low, close to the mic (Adam, lower tempo ~0.95), long silences.
- Look: night cinematics. Illustrated silhouettes walking through moonlit fog, a torch beam sweeping trees, layered forest depth, slow push-ins, deep blue-black with one red accent, heavy grain, vignette; real photos of the place and archive clippings where free; map pin at the start. Sound: low drone, footsteps, crickets that suddenly stop, heartbeat, silence before the cut.
- Never: invented facts or dialogue, real names of private victims in recent cases, gore.
